1.08 is the conventional sensible-air factor for standard-density air near sea level. These exercises assume resistance heating and steady airflow. Actual equipment settings and measurements must follow manufacturer instructions.

Worked example

A 5 kW heater produces 5 × 1,000 × 3.413 = 17,065 Btu/h. With a 22°F rise, airflow = 17,065 ÷ (1.08 × 22) = 718 CFM, rounded.

Typical sequence of operation

A thermostat heat call energizes the sequencer through the control circuit. Timed contacts energize the heater stages and blower as designed. The limit and fusible link provide overtemperature protection. At the end of the call, heaters shut off and the blower may continue for a delay to remove stored heat.

Safety and troubleshooting

A restricted filter or slipping blower belt reduces airflow and increases temperature rise. The high limit normally opens before the higher-temperature fusible link. Never solve repeated fuse failures by installing a larger fuse. Electrical checks on energized equipment require appropriate training and equipment.

Two questions to discuss with the instructor

Electric Heat Quiz Q6: The supplied key accepts a broken fan wire and a bad connection at the sequencer. A failed sequencer blower contact can also be possible even when heater contacts operate. The circuit diagram determines whether it can be excluded.

Electric Heat Quiz Q10: The supplied key accepts the fan relay, sequencer, and contactor, but excludes the thermostat. A thermostat is technically a control device. The intended category may be load-switching devices. This quizzer explicitly identifies the supplied-key question.
