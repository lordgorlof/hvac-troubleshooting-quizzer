2 · Heat energy and measured heat rate

FindFormula
Heat energy, Q (Btu)Mass (lb) × specific heat [Btu/(lb·°F)] × temperature rise (°F)
Temperature-rise rate(Final temperature − initial temperature) ÷ elapsed minutes
Heat rate (Btu/h)Q × 60 ÷ elapsed minutes
Specific heatQ ÷ (mass × temperature rise)
Mass16 mass ounces = 1 pound; mass ounces are not fluid ounces

Example: 0.5 lb of water warms 20°F in 5 min. Q = 0.5 × 1 × 20 = 10 Btu. Heat rate = 10 × 60 ÷ 5 = 120 Btu/h.

Use water specific heat ≈ 1 Btu/(lb·°F) for these exercises. A solution’s specific heat and total mass can differ from pure water. Container heating and surrounding-air losses mean sample absorption is not the burner’s full output. Conduction questions concern heat moving through materials; no open-flame experiment is needed to use this quizzer.

4 · Gas laws

Always convert first: psia = psig + local atmospheric psi. °R = °F + 459.67. K = °C + 273.15. Keep the gas amount fixed and use consistent volume units. Pressures and temperatures in gas-law ratios must be absolute.

Watch the units: Btu is energy; Btu/h is a rate. kW is power; kWh is energy. Pounds of force in the work equation are not interchangeable with an SI mass without the appropriate force conversion. Reference examples assume idealized constant conditions.
